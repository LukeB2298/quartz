# Research Methods

Research methods in psychology encompass the tools and techniques used to systematically observe, measure, and analyze behavior and mental processes.

## Research designs

### Experimental designs
- **Between-subjects** — different participants in each condition
- **Within-subjects** — same participants in all conditions
- **Mixed design** — combination of between and within factors
- **Quasi-experimental** — no random assignment to conditions

### Non-experimental designs
- **Correlational** — examining relationships without manipulation
- **Observational** — naturalistic or structured observation
- **Case studies** — in-depth study of single individuals
- **Surveys** — self-report questionnaires and interviews

## Statistics

### Descriptive statistics
- **Measures of central tendency** — mean, median, mode
- **Measures of dispersion** — range, variance, standard deviation
- **Normal distribution** — the bell curve and its properties

### Inferential statistics
- **Null hypothesis significance testing (NHST)** — p-values, alpha levels
- **t-tests** — comparing means of two groups
- **ANOVA** — comparing means across multiple groups
  - One-way ANOVA, factorial ANOVA, repeated-measures ANOVA
- **Correlation** — Pearson's r, Spearman's rho
- **Regression** — simple linear, multiple regression
- **Chi-square** — testing categorical associations

### Effect sizes and power
- **Cohen's d** — standardized mean difference
- **Eta-squared / partial eta-squared** — ANOVA effect size
- **Statistical power** — probability of detecting a true effect
- **[[Type I and Type II Errors]]** — false positives and false negatives

## Ethics

Psychological research must adhere to ethical guidelines:

1. **Informed consent** — participants must agree knowingly
2. **Right to withdraw** — participants can leave at any time
3. **Confidentiality** — data must be anonymized
4. **Debriefing** — participants must be informed of the study's purpose afterward
5. **Minimization of harm** — physical and psychological

See the [[Declaration of Helsinki]] and APA Ethics Code for full guidelines.
