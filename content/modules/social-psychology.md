# Social Psychology

Social psychology examines how individuals think, feel, and behave in social contexts. It bridges psychology and sociology, focusing on the individual within groups rather than group-level analysis.

## Core topics

### Attitudes
- **[[ABC Model of Attitudes]]** — Affective, Behavioral, Cognitive components
- **[[Cognitive Dissonance]]** — Festinger's theory of attitude change
- **[[Elaboration Likelihood Model]]** — central vs. peripheral persuasion routes (Petty & Cacioppo)

### Social influence
- **[[Conformity]]** — Asch's line experiments
- **[[Obedience]]** — [[Milgram's Obedience Studies]]
- **[[Social Norms]]** — descriptive vs. injunctive norms

### Group dynamics
- **[[Social Facilitation]]** — improved performance in presence of others
- **[[Social Loafing]]** — reduced effort in groups
- **[[Groupthink]]** — Irving Janis's theory of faulty group decision-making
- **[[Bystander Effect]]** — Darley & Latané's diffusion of responsibility

### Social cognition
- **[[Attribution Theory]]** — internal vs. external attributions
- **[[Fundamental Attribution Error]]** — overestimating dispositional factors
- **[[Stereotypes and Prejudice]]** — [[Tajfel's Social Identity Theory]]

## Key studies

| Study | Researcher | Year | Key finding |
|---|---|---|---|
| Conformity experiments | Solomon Asch | 1951 | People conform to majority even when clearly wrong |
| Obedience experiments | Stanley Milgram | 1963 | Ordinary people will administer lethal shocks under authority |
| Stanford Prison Experiment | Philip Zimbardo | 1971 | Situational power can override individual disposition |
| Bystander intervention | Darley & Latané | 1968 | More bystanders = less likely any individual helps |
