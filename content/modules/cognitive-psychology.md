# Cognitive Psychology

Cognitive psychology is the study of mental processes — how we perceive, attend, remember, reason, and communicate. It emerged as a distinct field in the 1950s and 1960s, partly as a response to the limitations of [[behaviorism]].

## Core areas

### Perception
The process by which we organize and interpret sensory information. Key topics include:
- **Gestalt principles** — how we group visual elements
- **Top-down vs. bottom-up processing** — expectation vs. raw data
- **Depth perception** — monocular and binocular cues

### Attention
The selective focus on specific information while ignoring other inputs.
- **Selective attention** — [[Broadbent's Filter Theory]], [[Treisman's Attenuation Theory]]
- **Divided attention** — [[Kahneman's Capacity Model]]
- **The cocktail party effect** — attending to one conversation among many

### Memory
How information is encoded, stored, and retrieved.
- **[[Multi-Store Model]]** — sensory, short-term, and long-term memory (Atkinson & Shiffrin, 1968)
- **[[Working Memory]]** — Baddeley's model with phonological loop, visuospatial sketchpad, and central executive
- **[[Levels of Processing]]** — Craik & Lockhart's depth-of-processing framework
- **[[Episodic vs. Semantic Memory]]** — Tulving's distinction

### Language
How we comprehend and produce speech and text.
- **[[Broca's area]]** and **[[Wernicke's area]]** — language production and comprehension
- **[[Chomsky's Universal Grammar]]** — innate language acquisition device
- **[[Sapir-Whorf Hypothesis]]** — linguistic relativity

## Key experiments

| Experiment | Researcher(s) | Finding |
|---|---|---|
| Serial position curve | Ebbinghaus | Primacy and recency effects |
| The Magical Number Seven | Miller | Short-term memory capacity ~7±2 items |
| The War of the Ghosts | Bartlett | Memory is reconstructive, not reproductive |
| Visual search tasks | Treisman | Feature integration theory |
| Stroop effect | Stroop | Automaticity of reading |
