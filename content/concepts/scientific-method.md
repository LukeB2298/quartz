# The Scientific Method in Psychology

Psychology is a science. It uses the scientific method to build knowledge about behavior and mental processes through systematic observation, measurement, and experimentation.

## The process

1. **Observe** — notice a phenomenon or pattern of behavior
2. **Formulate a question** — what causes this behavior?
3. **Develop a hypothesis** — a testable, falsifiable prediction
4. **Design a study** — choose a method (experimental, correlational, observational)
5. **Collect data** — measure behavior using valid and reliable instruments
6. **Analyze** — use statistics to test the hypothesis
7. **Draw conclusions** — interpret results in light of theory and previous research
8. **Replicate** — other researchers repeat the study to verify findings

## Key principles

### Falsifiability
A scientific hypothesis must be testable and potentially disprovable (Popper). Claims that cannot be tested are not scientific.

### Operationalization
Defining variables in measurable terms. For example, "happiness" could be operationalized as a score on a well-being questionnaire.

### Validity and reliability
- **Validity** — the study measures what it claims to measure
  - Internal validity — confounds are controlled
  - External validity — results generalize beyond the study
- **Reliability** — results are consistent and replicable

## The replication crisis

In recent years, psychology has faced a **replication crisis**. Many classic findings have failed to replicate in new studies. This has led to:

- Pre-registration of studies
- Open data and open materials
- Larger sample sizes
- Greater emphasis on effect sizes over p-values
