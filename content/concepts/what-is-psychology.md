# What is Psychology?

Psychology is the scientific study of behavior and mental processes. It seeks to understand how people think, feel, and act, both individually and in groups.

## Major perspectives

| Perspective | Focus | Key figures |
|---|---|---|
| Biological | Brain, genes, nervous system | |
| Behavioral | Observable behavior, learning | Watson, Skinner |
| Cognitive | Mental processes — thinking, memory | |
| Psychodynamic | Unconscious drives, early experience | Freud, Jung |
| Humanistic | Free will, self-actualization | Maslow, Rogers |
| Social-cultural | Cultural and social influences on behavior | |

## Subfields

- [[Cognitive Psychology]] — perception, attention, memory, language
- [[Social Psychology]] — social influence, attitudes, group dynamics
- [[Developmental Psychology]] — lifespan development
- [[Biological Psychology]] — brain and behavior
- [[Research Methods]] — how psychologists gather and analyze data
- [[Abnormal Psychology]] — psychological disorders
- [[Personality Psychology]] — individual differences

## Why study psychology?

1. **Understand yourself** — gain insight into your own thoughts and behaviors
2. **Understand others** — improve relationships and communication
3. **Critical thinking** — learn to evaluate claims about behavior scientifically
4. **Practical applications** — from mental health to education, business, and law
